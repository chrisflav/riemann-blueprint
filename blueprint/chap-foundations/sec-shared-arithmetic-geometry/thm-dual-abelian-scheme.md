For every abelian scheme $A$ over a scheme $S$ there are an abelian scheme $B$
over $S$, of the same relative dimension, and a line bundle $P$ on $A \times_S B$
that makes $B$ the dual of $A$ and, with the factors exchanged, $A$ the dual
of $B$.

Over a field this is Mumford's theorem. Over a general base the classical
theorem (Artin; Faltings–Chai, Chapter I) gives the dual as an algebraic space,
and as a scheme when $A$ is projective locally on $S$; *we read* the statement as
asking for a scheme over every base.
