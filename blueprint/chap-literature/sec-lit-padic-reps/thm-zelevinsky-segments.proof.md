Each part comes from the Bernstein–Zelevinsky theory of
[thm-bz-finiteness]: the finite length of Jacquet modules
and induced representations ([thm-bz-functors-finite-length],
[thm-jacquet-irreducible-admissible]), the geometric lemma
([thm-geometric-lemma-filtration], [thm-geometric-lemma]) to compute Jacquet
modules of products, and the Borel–Casselman isomorphism
([thm-borel-casselman]) for the Iwahori-spherical case. The global
statement comes from the existence of Galois representations for
polarized pairs in the sense of [def-galois-reps-polarized], with the
local parameters matched to the Langlands classification of part 3 and
Henniart's local converse theorem [thm-henniart-local-converse].
