This chapter collects the published theorems that the proof uses, each in
the form in which it is used. It is a catalogue and has no main theorem:
the other chapters cite its entries in their proofs, never as hypotheses.
None of these theorems is assumed. Each comes with a proof, which the entry
sketches, though for many we only name what the proof draws on.

The heading of each entry names its source. An entry marked *published*
states the theorem of that source, perhaps in other notation or in the
setting the proof needs. An entry marked *modified* states a special case,
a weaker form or a variant, and says how it differs from the published
theorem. Two entries are marked *new*: the Hodge filtration of an abelian
scheme [lem-hodge-filtration-abelian-scheme], a step in the proof of the
de Rham theorem, and the semi-abelian models with theta functions
[thm-semi-abelian-theta-models], whose published source we could not
identify. Where we read a statement in a particular way, or cannot tell
what it means, we say so.

The fourteen sections go by subject. Six are arithmetic: class field
theory, complex multiplication, $L$-functions, algebraic Hecke characters,
Galois cohomology and $p$-adic Hodge theory. Two are geometric: abelian
schemes, and algebraic, complex and Arakelov geometry. Six concern
representations and automorphic forms: of $\GL_n$ over number fields,
over $p$-adic fields and in depth zero, of real unitary groups and of
unitary groups over number fields, and the Weil representation with the
doubling method. A few remarks list results that the proof uses together.

Some published theorems sit instead in the chapter that uses them most:
integral models, Mumford's degenerations, toroidal compactifications and
resolution of singularities in [chap-heights], base change for unitary
groups in [chap-galois], and the Siegel–Weil formula in [chap-theta].
