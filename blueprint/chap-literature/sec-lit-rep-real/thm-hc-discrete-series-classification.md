For each $n$ there is a family of representations $\pi_{\varepsilon,\lambda}$
of the groups $\U(p,q)$, $p + q = n$, indexed by a signature $\varepsilon$
and a parameter $\lambda$, such that for $\lambda$ strictly decreasing with
$\lambda_a - (n-1)/2 \in \Z$:

* $\pi_{\varepsilon,\lambda}$ is irreducible, unitary and square-integrable,
  with infinitesimal character $\lambda$;
* the $K$-type $\lambda + \rho_n - \rho_c$ occurs exactly once, and every
  $K$-type is this one plus a sum of positive roots (Blattner);

and every irreducible square-integrable unitary representation of a
$\U(p,q)$ is unitarily equivalent, after a permutation of coordinates, to
some $\pi_{\varepsilon,\lambda}$. This is Harish-Chandra's classification
(1965–66) with the Blattner formula of Hecht and Schmid (1975).
