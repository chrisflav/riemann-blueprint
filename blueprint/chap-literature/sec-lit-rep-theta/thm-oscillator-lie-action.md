For a non-empty finite index set $l$ there is a two-fold covering
$\mathrm{Mp}(l) \to \mathrm{Sp}(l, \R)$, with non-trivial kernel element
$\epsilon$, and a unitary representation $\omega$ of $\mathrm{Mp}(l)$ on
$L^2(\R^l)$ such that $\epsilon$ acts by $-1$, $\omega$ is strongly
continuous, intertwines the Heisenberg action, preserves Schwartz
functions, is smooth along smooth families in the group, and differentiates
on one-parameter subgroups $\exp(tX)$, $X \in \mathfrak{sp}$, to the quadratic
differential operator attached to $X$. This is the Weil representation with
its infinitesimal form.
