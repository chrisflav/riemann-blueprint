Write integers in base $B$ with $S$ digits, each digit small compared with
$B/L$, and keep those whose digit vector lies on a fixed sphere
$\sum_m v_m^2 = k$. A relation $j(s_2 - s_1) = i(s_3 - s_1)$ with small $i, j$
does not carry over the digits, so it holds for the digit vectors, and
three distinct points of a sphere cannot satisfy it. Choosing $k$ by the
pigeonhole principle and optimising $B$ and $S$ gives the size bound. For the
consequence, three points of $u_0 + Ta$ in a progression of length $L$ would
give such a relation, since the multiples involved are smaller than the
order of $a$; when $h$ has order at most $4L$ two points of a coset already
force equal elements of $T$.
