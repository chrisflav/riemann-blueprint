There is $C > 0$ such that for all $n \ge 1$ and $L \ge 2$ there is a set $T$
of integers $0 \le s < n/4L$ with
$$|T| \ge \frac{n}{L}\,e^{-C\sqrt{\log n\,\log L}},$$
such that no three distinct $s_1, s_2, s_3 \in T$ satisfy
$j(s_2 - s_1) = i(s_3 - s_1)$ with $0 < |i|, |j| \le L$ and $i \ne j$.

Consequently, in a cyclic group generated by an element $a$ of order $n$,
the translates $u_0 + Ta$ meet every arithmetic progression
$t, t + h, \dots, t + (L-1)h$ in at most two points when $h$ has order
greater than $4L$, and every coset $t + \langle h\rangle$ in at most one point
when $h$ has order at most $4L$.

The relation with $i = 1$, $j = 2$ says that $s_1, s_2, s_3$ are in
arithmetic progression, so $T$ is in particular free of three-term
progressions; Behrend's construction from integer points on a sphere gives
it. *We read* $h$ as a Frobenius element, and $T$ as a set of twists that
the orbits of Frobenius meet rarely.
