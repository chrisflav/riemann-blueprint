The first step is an *integral pairing for $\GL_r$*: for a dominant weight
$\alpha$ of $\GL_r$ with entries in $[0, N]$ and spread at most $c \le 8a$,
there is a $\GL_r$-invariant pairing between the integral models of the
representations of highest weights $\alpha$ and $N - \alpha^{\mathrm{rev}}$,
integral on them, and bases $u_b$, $u'_b$ of these models, as many as the
Weyl dimension of $\alpha$, on which the pairing is diagonal with entries
$p^{k_b}$, $k_b \le \lceil (r+1)^2(\log_p(8a + r + 1) + 1)\rceil$. It follows
from the Smith normal form of the relevant Gram matrices and the explicit
Weyl modules.

The pairing, with Cartan's surjectivity for the explicit map onto the space
in two letters, gives a contraction onto the standard lattice. The member's
admissibility conditions say that the graded lattices are stable, which is
what the contraction needs. The contraction, the injectivity of the explicit
map and Gauss inversion on the lattice in two letters then give the large
direct summand and the degree bound.
