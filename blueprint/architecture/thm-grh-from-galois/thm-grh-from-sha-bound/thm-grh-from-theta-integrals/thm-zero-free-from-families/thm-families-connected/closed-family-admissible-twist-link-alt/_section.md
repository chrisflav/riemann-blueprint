Let $\mathcal A$ be a class of families ([def-family-data]) with a choice of
twists for its members, and let $\mathcal A_3$ be the subclass of families
that carry designs of size constant $3$ and satisfy the divisibility,
tameness and counting conditions. Suppose that, for all small $\varepsilon$ and
all families in $\mathcal A_3$ that are large enough, the following hold
for all members at large $x$:

* the points are integral, before and after twisting;
* the sums of period classes obey Weyl's law, and the twisted periods are
  admissible;
* the sharpened column bounds hold, also at the twist;
* the tuples used are bounded by a constant $C_1$, and the size of the
  family at the prime of its type is bounded in terms of $C_1$;
* the choice of twists is *served*: each twisted member satisfies the twisted
  comparison and system conditions, and the kernels of the chosen twists
  satisfy the orbit, integrality and shape conditions at the inert and
  split places.

Then, in the same sense, every member of every family in $\mathcal A_3$
eventually satisfies the second admissibility condition of the family
hypotheses.
