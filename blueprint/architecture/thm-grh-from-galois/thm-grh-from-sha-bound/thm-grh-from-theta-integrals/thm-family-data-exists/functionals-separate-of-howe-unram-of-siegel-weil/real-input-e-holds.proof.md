Two statements combine. First, a large projection: some translate $\pi(g_0)v_0$
of the lowest $K$-type vector, with $\|g_0\|$ bounded polynomially in $B$, has
orthogonal projection to the $\sigma$-isotypic part of norm at least
$e^{-100(n+1)^4\log(8(n+1)B)}$. This uses a model of the lowest $K$-type, a
moment estimate and analytic bounds on matrix coefficients. Second, an
eigenvector argument: for a $\sigma$ of formal degree $d$ and a vector with
nonzero projection, the integral of matrix coefficients is a positive
multiple of $\langle b, w\rangle$, with constant at least the squared norm
of the projection divided by $2d$. Together they give $v$, $w'$ and $c$.
