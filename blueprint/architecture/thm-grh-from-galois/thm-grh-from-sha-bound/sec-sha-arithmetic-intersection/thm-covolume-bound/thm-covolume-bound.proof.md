By [thm-covolume-bound-from-heights] it is enough to bound the mean
arithmetic volumes of automorphic line bundles from below, and
[thm-mean-arithmetic-volume-lower-bound] does this. That lower bound rests on
resolution and principalisation ([thm-hironaka-resolution],
[thm-principalisation-relative]), the inverse Poincaré–Lelong formula
([thm-poincare-lelong-integrated]), the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel
inequalities and Moriwaki's theorem
([thm-arithmetic-hilbert-samuel-inequalities]), Fargues' exact sequence
([thm-fargues-hodge-tate-sequence]), Zarhin's theorem ([thm-zarhin-trick]) and
Riemann–Roch for nef bundles ([thm-hilbert-samuel-nef]). The height argument
uses the toroidal compactifications of Siegel moduli and of unitary Shimura
varieties ([thm-toroidal-compactification-siegel],
[thm-toroidal-compactification-unitary]) with their charts, seminegativity
of the Lie algebras of semi-abelian schemes ([thm-hodge-bundle-semipositive]),
Green's regular characters and classification
([thm-green-regular-characters], [thm-green-parametrisation]), separation of
points ([thm-angehrn-siu-point-separation]), arithmetic degrees
([thm-arithmetic-intersection-theory]), the published theorems on Hodge
bundles ([def-hodge-bundle-inputs]) and Grothendieck's deformation theorem
([thm-grothendieck-messing]). Semistable reduction, the norm theorems of
Hasse and Landherr, multiplication by $n$ on abelian schemes and Serre's
holomorphic sections enter through the toroidal charts.
