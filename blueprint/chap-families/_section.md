This chapter proves [thm-family-data-exists]: if cuspidal representations
of $\GL_a$ over CM fields have Galois representations, some class of
families of definite unitary groups satisfies the family hypotheses. The
zero-free region needs them: [thm-zero-free-from-families] takes such a
class, together with the Tate–Shafarevich bound, and shows
that primitive Dirichlet $L$-functions have no zeros right of $1/2$.

A member of a family is a chain of definite unitary groups
$\U(X_0) \to \dots \to \U(X_m)$ over an auxiliary CM field, of ranks
$k + j(t+1)$, whose consecutive groups are linked by theta lifts and carry
lattices, together with indefinite and auxiliary hermitian spaces, weights
of size $x\,e^{x^\varepsilon}$ at the archimedean places, and local data at
every prime. The family hypotheses ([def-family-data]) are about fifty
conditions on these lattices, periods, masses and spaces of automorphic
forms. The proof reaches them through a shorter list of 26 conditions
checked at each admissible datum of a family.

The proof has seven steps. Four are in this chapter. (1) Siegel's mass
formula for standard hermitian lattices gives the members and admissible
data for them. (2) Integral models of unitary Shimura varieties bound the
local data at the points of a family, and the archimedean weights are
bounded below. (3) A system of local parameters gives twelve basic
conditions at every admissible datum. (6) The basic conditions, with a
covolume bound and a functional condition, give the 26 conditions at every
admissible datum. Steps (4) and (5), on theta lifts and slopes on the indefinite
side, are in [chap-theta]; the covolume bound is in [chap-heights]; step
(7), the bound on the error term of a member, is in [chap-sha].
