A member of a family in [chap-families] is a chain of definite unitary
groups joined by theta lifts. To show that the families satisfy their
conditions we need several facts about these lifts: that they do not
vanish, that they separate vectors, that suitable test vectors have
algebraic values, how their norms depend on $L$-values, and how the periods
built from them behave. This chapter proves these facts. [thm-theta-inputs]
collects them.

The Siegel–Weil formula is the starting point: it identifies an average of
theta functions over an adelic unitary group with a value of a Siegel
Eisenstein series. Through the Rallis inner product formula it gives the
step identities of theta towers: each step up a tower multiplies the
constant by one special value of a Hecke $L$-function. From these come
separation of vectors by theta functionals, non-vanishing of downward
lifts, rational test vectors and, iterating along Witt towers, the slope
bound on the indefinite side. The isometric period identity then writes
the periods of a family as inner products of theta lifts with projections.

The last four sections give estimates. Bessel transforms on the real
unitary group of signature $(k+r, r+1)$ satisfy radial equations, which with
Capelli's identity give bounds for discrete series coefficients. These
enter the off-diagonal bound, which says that distinct types are almost
orthogonal. A kernel argument counts automorphic forms of each type, with
main term given by Siegel's mass formula. Katz's $p$-adic measures from CM
theta functions give valuation bounds at the level primes.

The proof of [thm-family-data-exists] uses these results in its steps on
theta lifts, on slopes and on the full list of family conditions.
